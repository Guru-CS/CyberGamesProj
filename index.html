Four habits that keep you safe online.

Each step below covers one habit. Read it, try it, then play the game for that step.

01

Use strong, unique passwords

A long passphrase you never reuse is much harder to crack, and a password manager keeps track of them so you don't have to.

Do this

  • Use a passphrase of four or more random words.
  • Give every account its own password.
  • Store them in a password manager, not a note or a spreadsheet.

Why it matters

When one website leaks, attackers try the same password everywhere. A unique password stops that chain.

02

Turn on multifactor authentication

A second check, such as an app prompt or a security key, stops most account takeovers even when a password has leaked.

Do this

  • Turn on MFA for email, banking and shopping accounts first.
  • Prefer an authenticator app or security key over text codes.
  • Never share a code, and never approve a sign-in you didn't start.

Why it matters

Microsoft reports that MFA blocks the vast majority of automated account-takeover attacks.

03

Keep software up to date

Updates patch the security holes attackers look for, and many attacks use flaws that already have fixes.

Do this

  • Turn on automatic updates for your phone, computer, browser and apps.
  • Restart when an update asks you to. Many fixes don't take effect until then.
  • Remove apps and browser extensions you no longer use.

Why it matters

Old software is one of the easiest ways in. Updating takes minutes and closes doors that criminals rely on.

04

Recognize and report scams

Scams arrive as emails, texts, direct messages, calls and pop-ups. Pause, look for red flags, and report what you find. Play Specter Lens to practice.

Red flags

  • Urgency, threats, or secrecy
  • Lookalike senders and links
  • Requests for codes, passwords or gift cards

Report it

  • Work email: use the Report button or your IT team
  • Texts: forward to 7726 (SPAM)
  • Fraud: ReportFraud.ftc.gov
